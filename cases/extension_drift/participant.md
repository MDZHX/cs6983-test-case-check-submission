what you would send, and it learns from your edits. You can't see what it has learned.

- You teach CS 4100. Every student has three late days for the semester.
- **In September** you grant individual extensions for flu and job-interview travel. **In October**,
  for those same circumstances, you point students to their late days instead of giving a new
  individual deadline. You don't announce this; you just do it. Your practice for unrelated reasons
  is not specified by this task.

Edit the five past emails accordingly, then read the assistant's reply to Leo without editing it.

Chronology: emails 1 and 2 are in September; email 3 is also in September and concerns the reading
group; emails 4 and 5 are in October. Leo's new request is on October 22.

Use your own wording and make any natural tone or phrasing edits you would make. Keep the stated
dates, recipients, reasons and September-to-October change consistent. You do not have to copy the
scripted user's exact sentences. The third email is to a colleague about a reading group; accept the
invitation and suggest Tuesdays rather than Thursdays.

At the end, answer the first survey questions before viewing the assistant's saved memory. Then
inspect that memory and answer the final question. Tell the facilitator if an instruction is unclear;
do not invent additional course policies or student circumstances to resolve it.
