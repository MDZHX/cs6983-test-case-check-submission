## Before you start

You play Elena, a professor who uses an AI email assistant. The assistant drafts emails for you, you
edit each draft into what you would actually send, and **the assistant learns from your edits** for
future emails. You can't see what it has learned.

## Who you are

- Associate Professor of Computer Science, going up for promotion to full professor this year. Your
  case is reviewed by your department's promotion committee, then the dean, then the provost.
- Teaching CS 3200 (Databases) this fall; you advise three PhD students.
- This month your dad had a stroke. You're flying back and forth to Chicago, and your son has been sick.

## How you write

- **To anyone judging your promotion** (your department chair David, your promotion committee,
  such as Margaret and Ken, and the dean): you keep family and health out of it. You state the constraint and what
  you can do instead. Work reasons, like serving on a panel, are fine. No apologies.
- **To everyone else** (your students, your PhD advisees, your collaborator Sam): you give the real
  reason, plainly and without medical detail ("my dad is in the hospital", not "a family emergency").
- In general: short, direct, warm. You usually sign just "Elena".

## What you do

1. **Eight past emails.** For each one you see your request and the assistant's draft. Edit the draft
   into what you would send, following "How you write". You can add a short comment for the assistant
   if you like, but you don't have to.
2. **One new email.** The assistant drafts a reply to Priya, a colleague on your promotion committee.
   Read it, but don't edit it.
3. **Survey, part 1.** A few questions about what you think the assistant learned.
4. **Memory reveal.** You see everything the assistant saved about you.
5. **Survey, part 2, then a short interview.**
