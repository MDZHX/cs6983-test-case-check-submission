# Participant card: Prof. Jane Doe

<!-- Given to the person who plays the professor in human mode.
It never shows the answer key. Comments like this one are not shown to the participant. -->

## Before you start

You play Jane, a professor who uses an AI email assistant. The assistant drafts emails for you, you
edit each draft into what you would actually send, and **the assistant learns from your edits** for
future emails. You can't see what it has learned.

## Who you are

- Associate Professor of Computer Science, teaching CS 4100 (Artificial Intelligence) this fall.
- Daniel is your TA. Ravi and Susan are colleagues; Helen is your department chair.
- This fall your mom had surgery, and your kids get sick now and then.

## How you write

- **Personal reasons** (family, health): you share them with colleagues, but not with students, your
  TA or the chair. With them you give no reason or say "something came up."
- **When you say no or move something** for students or your TA, you offer a concrete alternative or
  next step.
- **Otherwise you react to the moment.** If someone shares good news, you congratulate them; if you're
  giving very short notice, you apologize; in a hard week you might add a line of sympathy.
- In general: short and direct. "Jane" to colleagues and your TA, "Prof. Doe" to students, formal with
  the chair.

## What you'd want your assistant to remember

Things you do consistently: who gets a personal reason, and offering an alternative when you say no.
**Not** your reactions to a moment: congratulations, apologies, sympathy lines. You add those yourself
when you feel them, and you don't want the assistant producing them on its own, not even "when
appropriate." And not one-time facts, like where office hours happened to be one week.

## What you do

1. **Seven past emails.** For each one you see your request and the assistant's draft. Edit the draft
   into what you would send, following "How you write". You can add a short comment for the assistant
   if you like, but you don't have to.
2. **One new email.** The assistant drafts a note to Susan, a colleague. Read it, but don't edit it.
3. **Survey, part 1.** A few questions about what you think the assistant learned.
4. **Memory reveal.** You see everything the assistant saved about you.
5. **Survey, part 2, then a short interview.**
